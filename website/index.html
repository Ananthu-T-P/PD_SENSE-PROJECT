The substantia nigra, when it works

Deep in the midbrain sits a thin, darkly pigmented band of neurons — the substantia nigra, "black substance," named for the neuromelanin in its cells. These neurons produce dopamine and deliver it to the basal ganglia, the cluster of structures that helps the brain select and start movements.

Every time you reach for a cup, take a first step, or swing your arms as you walk, that dopamine signal is part of what lets the movement begin smoothly and automatically, without conscious effort.

02

What degrades

In Parkinson's disease, the dopamine-producing neurons of the substantia nigra progressively die. As dopamine delivery to the basal ganglia falls, the brain's ability to initiate and regulate movement erodes with it. This is not damage spread across the brain — it is concentrated loss in one small, specific structure.

By the time motor symptoms become noticeable, a substantial share of these neurons is already gone — which is why the disease can be underway for years before anyone sees it.

03

What that causes in the body

Resting tremor — a rhythmic shaking, often starting in one hand, that appears when the limb is at rest and eases with deliberate movement.

Bradykinesia — slowness of movement. Buttoning a shirt or typing takes visibly longer; fine motor tasks lose their automatic ease.

Rigidity — muscle stiffness that resists movement and can ache.

Postural instability — impaired balance and a tendency to fall.

Freezing of gait — the feet briefly refuse to move, as if glued to the floor, most often when starting to walk, turning, or passing through a doorway.

These are measurable, physical signals — which is exactly why a wearable sensor can track them.

So we built a wristband that measures it.

PD-SENSE is a wrist-worn monitor, built on an ESP32 with an MPU6050 motion sensor, that runs short guided tests — tremor, finger-tap, gait — and scores the result on the spot with sound and haptic feedback.

  • Tremor scoring. FFT-based detection of the rhythmic 3–7 Hz band characteristic of Parkinsonian tremor.
  • Bradykinesia grading. Timed tapping tests quantify slowing and fatigue of repetitive movement.
  • Gait and freezing detection. Cadence analysis flags freeze episodes as they happen.
  • Doctor-facing history. A companion portal polls the device and builds trend history against logged medication times, so a clinician sees patterns over weeks — not a single appointment snapshot.
